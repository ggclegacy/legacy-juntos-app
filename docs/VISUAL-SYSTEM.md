# Luminous Brazil: visual implementation

The redesign translates Brazil's green, yellow, blue and white into fresh emerald, reflective gold, sapphire accents and pearl-white typography on obsidian. These are original product colors, not claims of official flag color codes. Brazilian modernist landscape design informs flowing geometry and tropical depth; no claim is made that one visual style represents every Brazilian person's taste.

## Research translated into construction

- MDN documents `backdrop-filter` as filtering the backdrop behind a translucent surface. Glass therefore requires both a translucent fill and something behind it to filter. The application combines ambient emerald/sapphire lighting, partially translucent layered backgrounds, bright top edges, inner shadows and restrained blur. Borders and shadows give stable depth even without blur. [MDN: backdrop-filter](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/Properties/backdrop-filter)
- web.dev recommends prioritizing transform and opacity animations rather than properties that trigger layout/paint. Card tilt uses shallow perspective transforms and requestAnimationFrame updates, with no per-frame React state. The moving highlight repaints only the active small card. There is no continuous WebGL render loop or animated full-screen blur. [web.dev: high-performance animations](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)
- Transform-based depth applies to the card plane; decorative glass and metallic highlights provide the rest of the material cue. The enhancement activates only for a fine pointer with hover and no reduced-motion preference. Touch retains a dimensional resting surface and normal scrolling. [WebKit: transform style](https://webkit.org/blog-files/3d-transforms/transform-style.html)
- Reduced transparency is a useful but not universally supported preference. The stylesheet responds with opaque surfaces where supported and also includes an unsupported-backdrop-filter fallback. Contrast does not depend on the media feature: ordinary text surfaces already have substantial dark tint. [MDN: reduced transparency](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@media/prefers-reduced-transparency)
- Burle Marx's landscape work combines sinuous forms, dramatic color contrasts and native tropical planting. The commissioned hero artwork interprets those principles as emerald glass and gold ribbons above a stylized tropical landscape; it is generated artwork, not a photograph of a real place or a copy of a specific work. [UNESCO](https://whc.unesco.org/en/list/1620/)
- Brazil's flag supplies the cultural color starting point. Gold is the app's metallic interpretation of yellow. [Brazilian government](https://www.gov.br/planalto/pt-br/conheca-a-presidencia/biblioteca-da-pr/simbolos-nacionais/bandeira/bandeira-nacional)

## Delivery approach

`materials.css` holds the coherent material, typography, color and responsive presentation layer; the existing layout and business logic remain in their established modules. Manrope and Cormorant Garamond are bundled locally through pinned Fontsource packages, avoiding third-party font requests. Artwork is stored as an optimized local WebP. New decorative Portuguese phrases carry `lang="pt-BR"`; English remains the UI language. This is cultural detail, not a claim of completed Portuguese localization.

The whole system includes navigation, buttons, lists, cards, Faith, Performance, Business, Studio, Vision, dialogs, assistant, login, and mobile surfaces. Glass is selective: mobile removes costly blur from full-screen surfaces. Motion and transparency preferences and forced-colors mode have explicit fallbacks. Existing privacy, authentication, data access, and provider controls are unchanged.
